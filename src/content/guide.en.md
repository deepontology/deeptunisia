{#intro}

DeepTunisia is an instrument, not a page. The claims it makes are drawn from verifiable evidence, and the interface is built so you can always tell which claim rests on what. This guide takes about five minutes: it explains the one rule that governs everything, the sections the interface splits into, how time works here, and which view answers which question.

## The one rule: an inference is never a fact {#rule}

Every claim in the dataset is filed under one of four epistemic bases. They never render identically, because they are not the same kind of fact:

{#bases}

## The record and the argument {#bubbles}

The top bar splits the interface into three sections. The distinction is the project's whole claim, stated before you open anything:

- **Graph** — the sourced record. Positions, relationships, events, and the evidence behind them. Everything here carries a basis and a citation.
- **Media** — the reading surfaces: in-house investigations built from the evidence system, and the third-party news feed. Investigations carry the same graded vocabulary as the record; the feed carries no basis and says so on the page.
- **Agora** — the community layer: discussion, proposed changes and reports. No basis, no source grade, no verification. It is deliberately rendered in a register no sourced claim ever wears.

Crossing between sections is cheap and safe. The dots next to the names are load-bearing: filled means "this is the record", hollow means "this is not".

## Time is a dimension here {#time}

The dock at the bottom is the timeline. Scrub it and every view projects onto that instant — the network shows who was live in 1994, the Chronicle shows who held which post. Two things to know:

- The **density curve** behind the track shows how much of the record exists at each date. It thins before 1987 not because less happened, but because the archive is thinner. That is the project's honesty signal: the map shows the record, not the past.
- A position is an **interval**, not a line in a table. "X held post Y" and "between these dates" are separate claims with separate standing — one can be documented while the other is inferred.

## What "verified" means {#verified}

The project's architecture is machines propose, humans verify. At this stage most records have not been through a second pair of eyes — the real number is published on the [about page](/about) rather than hidden. A record flagged "awaiting a primary source" is not an accusation; it is a to-do list the project shows you.

## If something is wrong {#correct}

The useful correction is a primary source: a decree number, a gazette reference, an official biography, a corporate filing. The [open questions](/evidence) are the current research agenda, and closing one is the fastest way to improve this map. The [open data](/data) page has the full graph, so you can check the reasoning rather than take it on trust.
