# How to read this article

This is the second DeepTunisia investigation: how many fake accounts existed in Tunisia from 2016 to 2026, who claimed each number and by what method. Every figure carries a grade [C001] and a source. Interpretations render as panels.

Bot is public shorthand, not a finding that every counted account was automated. Sources use different labels: fake accounts, CIB, Pages, sock-puppets, hacked accounts. Where a source does not establish automation, this investigation does not upgrade the label [C031].

## Evidence badges

- **documented** -- platform report or primary record [C005]
- **reported** -- journalism or single researcher relaying a count that could not be verified against a platform disclosure [C012]
- **unsubstantiated** -- circulating without published count, kept with attributed holder [C017]
- **disputed** -- an exclamation mark on the claim chip, with both holdings in the ledger [C010]

**Claim references** appear as [C005] linking to the ledger in `evidence.yaml`. **Entity mentions** appear as @ureputation. **Interpretation panels** are set after the prose as blockquotes.

## The honest sentence

In the 10-year window two platform-confirmed, Tunisia-linked takedowns name a countable number [C005][C001]. All else is illustration or gap.

<section_id: S02>
title: "Archimedes: the first trace"
type: narrative
---

On 16 May 2019 Facebook announced it had removed 265 Facebook and Instagram accounts, Pages, Groups and events originating in Israel and focused on six African countries including Tunisia [C001]. The split was 65 accounts, 161 Pages, 23 Groups, 12 events and four Instagram accounts [C001]. About 2.8 million accounts followed a Page, about 5,500 had joined a Group and about 920 followed an IG account [C001]. Spend was about $812,000 from December 2012 to April 2019 in Brazilian reals, Israeli shekel and dollars [C001]. Tunisia's share of the 265 was never published [C004].

Inkyfada counted the Tunisia subset via the DFRLab dataset: eleven Pages created between 17 January and 12 March 2019 [C002], together holding about 500,000 followers [C002]. Their 359 posts generated more than one million interactions and their 36 videos gathered near eight million views [C002]. That content criticised many politicians but never attacked Nabil Karoui [C002]; five Pages shared Nessma TV or Khalil Tounes material [C002]. The count is of Pages only. Do not read it as an account count [C002].

A different lens comes from the monitors. Between 15 May and 15 July 2019 @atide and @dri-democracy-reporting found that unofficial Pages with no declared affiliation, self-categorised as entertainment or satirical, produced 38.5 percent of political messages in their sample [C003]. They called clusters sharing similar content within a time window a page network [C003]. That figure is a share of messages, not an enumeration of bots [C003].

The same Archimedes operation ran a Tunisian-facing fake watchdog. @dfrlab-atlantic-council documents a Page called Stop a la desinformation et aux mensonges (Stop Disinformation and Lies), created March 2019 and quickly amassing more than 11,000 followers, which posed as a media watchdog while itself being inauthentic [C030]. It illustrates the pattern DFRLab flags for Archimedes worldwide: fake locals, then fake newsrooms and leak pages, then fake watchdogs [C030].

Tunisia was not only a place where a domestic farm was built. It was also a target. Four foreign-origin takedowns list Tunisia among their targets. Each total is global. Tunisia's share in each was never published and is marked here as ? [C031].

- Iran, 31 Jan 2019: 783 assets globally including Tunisia. 262 Pages plus 356 accounts plus 3 Groups plus 162 Instagram accounts equals 783 [C025]. Tunisia share is ? [C025].
- Egypt linked to El Fagr, 3 Oct 2019: 251 assets globally including Tunisia. 163 accounts plus 51 Pages plus 33 Groups plus 4 Instagram accounts equals 251 [C028]. Tunisia share is ? [C028].
- Iran linked to IRIB, 5 May 2020: 540 assets globally including Tunisia. 118 Pages plus 389 accounts plus 27 Groups plus 6 Instagram accounts equals 540 [C026]. Tunisia share is ? [C026].
- Egypt, Turkey and Morocco linked to the Muslim Brotherhood, 5 Nov 2020: 58 assets globally including Tunisia. 31 accounts plus 25 Pages plus 2 Instagram accounts equals 58 [C027]. Tunisia share is ? [C027].

These are not Tunisia counts. The 783 is 262 plus 356 plus 3 plus 162 global [C025]. The 540 is 118 plus 389 plus 27 plus 6 global [C026]. The 58 is 31 plus 25 plus 2 global [C027]. The 251 is 163 plus 51 plus 33 plus 4 global [C028]. Do not sum them with each other or with the 993 domestic Carthage total to make a cumulative bot total for 2019 to 2020 [C031]. Freedom House relays the April 2020 Iran figure in the same qualified form [C029].

<section_id: S04>
title: "Carthage: the countable farm"
type: narrative
---

The countable farm arrived a year later, and it is the only Tunisia-origin network with a discrete account count [C005]. On 5 June 2020 Facebook announced it had removed 993 assets originating in Tunisia and focused on Francophone Sub-Saharan Africa: 446 Pages, 182 Facebook accounts, 96 Groups, 60 events and 209 Instagram accounts [C005]. About 3.8 million accounts followed a Page, about 131,900 had joined a Group and about 171,500 followed an IG account [C005]. Sixty events drew up to 4,300 interested [C005]. Ad spend was about $331,000 primarily in euros and dollars [C005]. The network used fake accounts to masquerade as locals, like its own content and drive traffic off-platform [C005][C009].

Facebook said it found the network and linked it to the Tunisia-based PR firm @ureputation [C005]. @dfrlab-atlantic-council, alerted in September 2019, published a 30-page open-source investigation the same day [C009]. It reviewed more than five hundred Pages, Groups and accounts and showed the operation targeted ten countries: Chad, Comoros, Congo-Brazzaville, Cote d'Ivoire, Gambia, Guinea, Mali, Niger, Senegal and Tunisia, and produced election content for @nabil-karoui in Tunisia in 2019 [C006], Faure Gnassingbe in Togo in February 2020 [C006], Henri Konan Bedie in Cote d'Ivoire in October 2020 [C006] and Azali Assoumani in the Comoros in March 2019 [C006]. Francophone sites such as Revue de l'Afrique, registered May 2014, Afrika News and Maghreb-Info had operated for more than five years [C007].

The infrastructure trail is an address: Avenue Roseleo, Barcelona in WHOIS for kaissaied.com is a near-identical misspelling of Carrer del Rossello 218, the address of @lotfi-bel-hadj and his Digital Big Brother incubator, identified via the National Registry of Enterprises [C008]. The persona marking the enterprise is Fake News Checking, a Facebook page launched 29 August 2019 whose co-founder @moez-bhar cites ureputation.net since June 2014 [C008]. That trail is open-source linkage, not identity proof alone [C008].

Only 182 of the 993 is the Facebook-account count [C005].

<section_id: S05>
title: "The price: collateral"
type: narrative
---

This section is kept as a full section, not a sidebar, because enforcement has costs that calibrate trust in the next takedown [C010].

On 29 to 30 May 2020 up to 60 accounts and Pages belonging to journalists, bloggers and artists were deactivated without warning [C010], including Haythem El Mekki, Sarah Ben Hamadi and Bendir Man, plus the participative web radio Radyoon active since 2012 [C010]. Message: you are not eligible to use Facebook, decision is final [C010]. Both @article-19 and @access-now list the same names and quote the same denial from Radyoon: the deleted pages are not pages related to @ureputation [C010]. About twenty accounts reappeared within 24 hours [C010]; at least 28 were restored via Trusted Partner I Watch and the Access Now Helpline [C011].

Facebook told the Guardian on 4 June it was a small number removed by technical error and now restored [C011]. On 5 June its CIB Report said it had deliberately removed 446 Pages, 182 accounts, 96 Groups, 60 events and 209 Instagram accounts linked to @ureputation [C005][C011]. Whether the 29 to 30 May window is the same as or collateral to that removal remains open [C011]. No reconciliation was published [C011].

<section_id: S06>
title: "Hijack: a different playbook"
type: narrative
---

Three weeks after President Kais Saied dismissed the government on 25 July 2021, a different amplification surfaced. Not a domestic farm but evidence that Tunisia can be the target [C012]. An analysis of the hashtag Tunisians revolt against the brotherhood looked at 12,000 tweets from 6,800 unique accounts [C012]. The majority reported location as Saudi Arabia or the United Arab Emirates [C012]. The ten most influential accounts were all Gulf-based influencers including monther72, faljubairi, s_hm2030 and emarati_shield [C012].

The signal was not geography alone. One persona, Fairuz, was retweeted more than 200 times within five minutes after posting an unrelated comic video [C013]. More than 200 accounts retweeted that single tweet [C013]. A graph showed them as a disconnected constellation far from the main Saudi-Emirati cluster, a shape the researcher reads as inorganic [C013]. Many were reused hacked handles [C013].

Do not read 6,800 as 6,800 bots [C013]. The source distinguishes a real influencer core from a sock-puppet burst [C013]. 6,800 is not 6,800 bots. 6,800 is unique accounts in the hashtag sample. More than 200 is the sock-puppet burst retweeting one persona within five minutes [C013]. Do not read a foreign hijack as proof that the domestic 2020 farm was still running.

<section_id: S07>
title: "Decree 54 and the gap"
type: narrative
---

This section is also kept full because it is how the state now defines a fake [C015]. Decree Law 54 of 13 September 2022 creates three things the earlier years lacked: a crime for producing or spreading false news or rumours via information systems [C015], Article 24: five years and 50,000 dinars, doubled if the victim is a public official [C015], a retention floor of at least two years for data [C015], and corporate liability [C015]. The text contains no bot count [C015].

The capability had arrived a year earlier: on 24 March 2021 UNODC delivered $200,000 worth of forensic equipment and software to the Ministry of Interior [C016]. That value is not a scale [C016].

The claim that circulates is qualitative. Arab Center Washington and the POMED Q&A with Amine Snoussi both put it in the same words: heavy digital presence on Facebook, about seventy percent penetration, attacking and doxing individuals once the president has named them, with addresses and judicial leaks preceding arrests [C017]. No number is published in either piece [C017].

For 2021 to 2026 no Tier 1 or Tier 2 source enumerates a domestic farm of stated size [C018]. Meta and X reports through 2025 add no Tunisia domestic count [C018]. Digital Action ahead of the 6 October 2024 election describes pages and groups with tens of thousands of followers managed mostly from abroad, without counting bots [C019]. Arab Facts Hub's scrape of more than 150,000 posts finds hundreds of pro-Saied entities, without enumerating fakes [C019]. The popular digital army narrative is kept with its holders named. Its scale is kept as ? [C017].

<section_id: S08>
title: "How to count"
type: narrative
---

Four methods produce four different numbers, and they must not be summed [C024]. Platform CIB detection counts assets the platform removed [C024]. OSINT Page review counts Pages and sites the analyst could corroborate [C024]. Sample monitoring counts a share of messages inside a sample [C024]. Hashtag science counts tweets and handles inside one hashtag plus graph shape and velocity [C024].

That is why the only two countable takedowns differ from the illustrative figures beside them: 265 includes Tunisia, 993 originates in Tunisia [C001][C005]; 11 is Pages only [C002]; 38.5 percent is a prevalence [C003]; 12,000 is tweets inside one hijack [C012]. The same do-not-sum rule governs the foreign-targeting totals: 783, 540, 58 and 251 are each global and include Tunisia, but none is a Tunisia count and none sums with 993 [C031].
