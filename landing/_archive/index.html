I

Whoever controls information shapes the future.

Every society is built on memory. Memory is made of records; records are made of information.

For centuries the records were incomplete, the decisions hidden, and power difficult to understand — and harder still to challenge. That excuse no longer exists. We produce more information in a single day than entire generations once created in a lifetime. Yet understanding remains fragmented: facts are scattered, records disappear, connections stay invisible, public memory fades.

And wherever memory fades, power escapes accountability.

II

A single appointment means little until it is connected to an institution.

Deep Tunisia is a living public record of the people, institutions, companies and networks that shape the nation. It maps relationships, documents appointments, preserves timelines, records actions, connects evidence — transforming isolated facts into public knowledge.

Facts rarely exist in isolation. A company means little until its ownership is understood. A decision means little until it is placed in its historical context. Deep Tunisia exists to connect those fragments into a public understanding that no single document can provide.

Knowledge should never require permission.

III

Every claim should be traceable. Every conclusion should be challengeable.

Every claim in the record is filed under one of four bases — and they never render identically. An inference is never allowed to become a fact. Unsubstantiated claims are kept, not deleted: a map that only shows what it can prove cannot show why the popular account is wrong.

The four bases

Documented
stated in the primary record — a decree, a gazette, a court ruling
Reported
stated by credible outlets, not yet checked against the primary record
Inferred
reasoned from documented structure, with its reasoning and its falsifier attached
Unsubstantiated
circulating with no evidence — recorded, because deleting it would only hide why it circulates

Of 656 reviewable claims, 24 have been fully reviewed. The rest are published anyway — that is the system working, not failing.

IV

Information alone does not create a free society.

A nation also needs a place where its people can think together. Deep Tunisia is more than an archive — it is Tunisia's digital Agora. Not a place to replace institutions, but a place where citizens can understand them, question them, and improve the public record together.

Disagreements are settled with evidence instead of rumors; every voice carries the same weight.

Your identity is a 12-word key

Generated on your device from the standard BIP-39 word list. Never stored on a server. No email, no password, no profile to create or forget. The key IS your identity. Lose it, and your voice is gone. There is no recovery, because there is nothing to recover from.

01

Weekly posting budget

Four posts a week to start with — more as your standing grows. Citing a source costs half a post. This is deliberate, not a technical limit: it is a place for things you have thought about, not a chat.

02

10-minute hold

Every post waits ten minutes before it publishes. Only you can see it in that window; change your mind and it is refunded in full. Publish, and it enters the permanent record.

03

Nothing is silently deleted

Removed posts keep their place, their thread, their timestamp, and their reason. The body is nulled — but the record of the removal remains visible.

We cannot see who you are. But this runs on ordinary hosting, and like every host, the service receives your connection. If you post under the same identity for a long time, the way you write can identify you no matter how good the cryptography. That is the threat model, stated plainly.

V

Citizens should never be forced to choose between participation and personal safety.

Participation is pseudonymous by design: a key, not an account. No name, no email, no profile — nothing is asked of you, so nothing about you is stored anywhere.

Anonymity is engineered, not promised. Every layer that cannot hold is named on this page rather than hidden in fine print, because a promise the system cannot keep is a trap.

For funders and partners

The state's attempt at collective memory was sealed. This one cannot be.

In 2018 the official Truth and Dignity Institution — the state's own answer to "never forget" — was shut down and its archives were sealed. Deep Tunisia is the non-state, survivable alternative: an open-source record that exists independently of any government, any host, any single person.

Verification, not vibes. Funding goes to the slow work: primary sources from the Gazette, translation of the record, review of the remaining claims, and the infrastructure that keeps a public archive independent.

What this is

An open-source, source-backed atlas of political, military, security and economic power in Tunisia, 1956–2026. What it is not: a claim that a hidden government runs the country — a hypothesis the record currently fails.

What this is not

Not a website exposing a "deep state". That framing presupposes its conclusion. The narrower question is answerable: which institutions, individuals and economic groups retain influence when the formal regime changes — and which relationships are genuinely continuous rather than reconstructed after each rupture.

Open by design

Every claim is traceable, every change published, every source cited. Two obligations are enforced by the build: an inference may never become a fact, and no claim ships without a source.
